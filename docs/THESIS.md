# Two-year thesis

**Autonomy will become a credit score, and the warrant will be its unit of account.**

Today, "agent safety" means a system prompt and a confidence number nobody calibrated. That ends within two years for
a boring reason: insurance. Once agents move money, ship code and moderate people, someone has to underwrite the
losses, and underwriters don't price vibes. They price calibration histories.

Three shifts follow.

**1. Confidence scores die; priced regret survives.** Raw model confidence is uncalibrated and gameable. Decision layers
will standardise on expected-loss tables with explicit reversibility and blast radius, the same inputs an actuary already
understands.

**2. Actions become signed objects.** The unit of agent work stops being "a tool call" and becomes a tool call plus a
warrant: who authorised it, under which policy version, on what evidence. Tools will refuse unsigned calls the way
browsers refuse unsigned TLS. That makes agent liability auditable and transferable.

**3. Autonomy is earned, per domain, and revocable.** An agent holds L3 on refunds under $300 and L0 on payroll, and it
demotes itself when its Brier score slips. Platforms will publish autonomy ledgers the way they publish uptime.

The losers will treat "ask a human" as a failure mode. The winners will understand that *defer* and *escalate* are
features: the cheapest action in a high-stakes world is the one you correctly didn't take. Whoever owns the decision
layer owns the trust layer, and the trust layer is where the liability, and therefore the money, will sit.
